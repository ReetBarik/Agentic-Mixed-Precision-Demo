## Idioms to detect

- `catastrophic_cancellation`: subtraction of two nearly equal values
  (e.g. `(a + b) - a` when `b << a`, or `x^2 - y^2` when `x ≈ y`).
- `naive_variance`: computing variance as `E[X^2] - E[X]^2`, which cancels
  catastrophically when the variance is small relative to the mean.
- `log_sum_exp_naive`: computing `log(exp(a) + exp(b))` directly, which
  overflows or loses precision for large inputs.  Stable form uses max-shift.
- `large_magnitude_sum`: summing values of wildly different magnitudes where
  small terms are lost to round-off (e.g. adding 1e-15 to 1e15).
- `division_by_near_zero`: dividing by a quantity that may be close to zero,
  amplifying errors unboundedly.

## Severity guide

- `high`: the idiom will cause large errors across essentially all inputs in
  the declared ranges.
- `medium`: the idiom causes large errors for a significant subset of inputs.
- `low`: the idiom is present but its impact is limited (e.g. the term is
  small, the result is re-normalised, etc.).
